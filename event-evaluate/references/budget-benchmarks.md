## Break-Even Math Framework

### The Core Formula

```
Break-even deals = Total Event Cost ÷ Average ACV
Qualified conversations needed = Break-even deals ÷ Event-to-Close Rate
```

**Event-to-Close Rate benchmarks** (what % of qualified event leads eventually close):
- High-touch events (dinners, workshops): 20-30%
- Mid-size conferences with strong outreach: 15-25%
- Major conferences with booth only: 10-15%
- Major conferences with full playbook (outreach + booth + side event): 20-25%

### Example Calculations

| ACV | Event Cost | Break-even Deals | At 20% Close Rate: Qualified Convos Needed | Realistic? |
|-----|-----------|-----------------|-------------------------------------------|-----------|
| $10K | $15K | 1.5 deals | 8 qualified conversations | Very achievable |
| $10K | $50K | 5 deals | 25 qualified conversations | Achievable with strong outreach |
| $25K | $50K | 2 deals | 10 qualified conversations | Very achievable |
| $25K | $100K | 4 deals | 20 qualified conversations | Achievable for major conferences |
| $50K | $75K | 1.5 deals | 8 qualified conversations | Very achievable |
| $50K | $150K | 3 deals | 15 qualified conversations | Achievable with premium presence |
| $100K | $100K | 1 deal | 5 qualified conversations | Very achievable |
| $100K | $200K | 2 deals | 10 qualified conversations | Achievable |

### Pipeline-to-Cost Ratio Targets

| Ratio | Assessment |
|-------|-----------|
| 10x+ | Strong performer. This event clearly paid for itself. |
| 5-10x | Solid return. Worth repeating with optimization. |
| 3-5x | Marginal. Review execution — was this a planning issue or a fit issue? |
| Under 3x | Poor return. Either fix the fundamentals or reallocate the budget. |

**Important context:** These ratios measure *pipeline created*, not *revenue closed*. A $50K event that generates $500K in pipeline (10x) might only close $125K in revenue (2.5x on revenue). That's still a strong result for most B2B SaaS companies.

## Activity Benchmarks

What "good" looks like for event activities:

| Metric | Target Range | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| Pre-event meetings booked | 15-25 (major) / 5-10 (mid) | Through 6-touch outreach sequence |
| Qualified booth conversations per day | 15-25 (per 2-person team) | Score 3+ on the lead scoring rubric |
| Total qualified conversations (3-day show) | 40-80 | Across all team members |
| Follow-ups sent within 48 hours | 100% of score 3-4 leads | Non-negotiable SLA |
| Post-event meetings booked | 20-30% of score 3+ leads | Within 2 weeks of event |
| Pipeline created within 30 days | Your $ target | Track opportunities, not just leads |

## Budget Red Flags

Flag these when evaluating:

- **Spending more than 30% of quarterly marketing budget on a single event.** Unless you're all-in on events as a channel, this is concentration risk.
- **No budget for pre-event outreach.** The outreach sequence is where 40-50% of qualified meetings come from. Skipping it to save $2K on marketing is false economy.
- **No budget for a side event.** For $50K+ total spend, a $5K-$10K dinner generates disproportionate pipeline. Intimate settings close deals faster than booth conversations.
- **Travel budget assumes sharing rooms or skipping meals.** Tired, hungry reps underperform. Budget for single rooms and team meals. This is not where you cut costs.
- **Booth budget under $3K for a major conference.** A cheap-looking booth at an expensive conference is worse than no booth. You're signaling "we can't afford to be here."
