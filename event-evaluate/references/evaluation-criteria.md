# Event Evaluation Criteria

## 6-Dimension Scoring Rubric

Score each dimension 1-5. Total out of 30.

### 1. ICP Density (How concentrated is your buyer at this event?)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|---------|
| 5 | 60%+ of attendees match your ICP. This is *the* event for your buyers. Attendee list confirms heavy target account presence. |
| 4 | 40-60% ICP match. Strong overlap but not exclusively your buyers. You'll have plenty of conversations worth having. |
| 3 | 20-40% ICP match. Your buyers attend but they're not the primary audience. You'll need to work harder to find them. |
| 2 | Under 20% ICP match. A handful of relevant people but you'll spend most of your time in low-value conversations. |
| 1 | Minimal ICP presence. This is not where your buyers go. Attending is a vanity play. |

**How to assess:** Check the attendee profile on the event website. Look at past attendee lists if available. Search LinkedIn for people who posted about attending last year — do they match your ICP? Check if target accounts have sponsored or exhibited before.

### 2. Budget-to-Opportunity Ratio (Can you afford enough presence to matter?)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|---------|
| 5 | Budget gives you a commanding presence (premium booth, speaking slot, hosted event). Your investment is proportional to the opportunity. |
| 4 | Budget gets you solid visibility. Mid-tier sponsorship with enough left for outreach and side events. |
| 3 | Budget is adequate but lean. Basic booth or small sponsorship. You can be there but won't stand out. |
| 2 | Budget is stretched thin. You're choosing between a booth and travel. Presence will feel underfunded. |
| 1 | Budget is insufficient. You can barely afford to attend, let alone make an impression. Better to skip and reallocate. |

**ACV-based guardrails:**
- Under $10K ACV: Don't spend more than $15K on any single event. Focus on high-density niche events.
- $10K-$50K ACV: Budget up to $50K for tier-1 events if pipeline math works. $10K-$25K for most.
- $50K-$150K ACV: $50K-$100K+ can pencil if you book 15+ qualified meetings.
- $150K+ ACV: Larger budgets justified — even 2-3 closed deals can cover a $150K+ event investment.

### 3. Content/Speaking Opportunity (Can you get on stage or create content?)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|---------|
| 5 | You have a confirmed speaking slot (keynote or breakout). Maximum authority positioning. |
| 4 | Speaking slot is available and your submission is competitive. Or you're co-presenting with a customer. |
| 3 | No speaking slot but good content opportunities — panel participation, podcast recordings, video content at the booth. |
| 2 | Limited content opportunities. You could do social media coverage but nothing structured. |
| 1 | No content angle. You'd be there purely for booth conversations. |

**Why this matters:** A speaking slot alone can justify event attendance even if other scores are mediocre. Being on stage positions you as a category leader, generates content for months after, and draws traffic to your booth.

### 4. Competitive Landscape (Are competitors there? Is that good or bad?)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|---------|
| 5 | You'd be the only (or one of few) vendors in your category. Massive differentiation opportunity. |
| 4 | Competitors are there but you have a clear differentiation story. Their presence validates the category and drives buyer interest. |
| 3 | Moderate competitive presence. Standard competitive dynamics — you need strong positioning but it's manageable. |
| 2 | Heavy competitive presence with larger-budget competitors. Risk of getting drowned out unless you have a standout strategy. |
| 1 | Dominated by competitors with 5-10x your budget. You'll look like the small player in the room. Consider whether that positioning helps or hurts. |

**Note:** A low score here isn't always a deal-breaker. If your ICP density is high (score 4-5), competitive presence can actually help — buyers are already in "evaluation mode." But if you're competing against well-funded incumbents at an event where your ICP presence is moderate, your money is better spent elsewhere.

### 5. Timing Alignment (Does this fit your sales cycle and roadmap?)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|---------|
| 5 | Event timing is perfect — aligns with your buying season, you have a new product/feature to announce, and pipeline from this event will close within your target quarter. |
| 4 | Good timing — event lands during an active buying period and you can execute the full pre/post event playbook (8+ weeks lead time). |
| 3 | Acceptable timing — not peak buying season but you have enough lead time to plan properly. Pipeline will close in the next quarter. |
| 2 | Challenging timing — less than 6 weeks until the event (rushed planning), or the pipeline won't close for 2+ quarters. |
| 1 | Bad timing — event conflicts with a major company milestone, you don't have enough lead time, or you're mid-reorg/rebrand and your story isn't ready. |

**Key consideration:** If your sales cycle is 90+ days, an event in Q4 generates pipeline that closes in Q1-Q2 of next year. Make sure your ROI measurement accounts for this lag.

### 6. Strategic Value (Beyond direct pipeline)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|---------|
| 5 | Category-defining event for your market. Not attending sends a signal. Multiple strategic angles: partnerships, analyst presence, media coverage, recruiting. |
| 4 | Strong strategic value — good for brand positioning, partner ecosystem presence, or analyst/media access. |
| 3 | Some strategic value — useful for industry presence but not a "must attend" from a brand perspective. |
| 2 | Limited strategic value beyond direct pipeline. If the pipeline math doesn't work, there's little reason to go. |
| 1 | No strategic angle. This is purely a pipeline play — evaluate it only on ICP density and budget math. |

---

## Scoring Thresholds

| Total Score | Recommendation | What to tell the team |
|------------|---------------|----------------------|
| 24-30 | **Strong Yes** | "This event checks every box. Commit budget, start planning immediately." |
| 18-23 | **Conditional Yes** | "Worth doing *if* [specific conditions]. Here's what needs to be true." |
| 12-17 | **Likely No** | "The math doesn't work at this budget. Consider alternatives." |
| Under 12 | **Hard No** | "Skip it. Reallocate the budget to [alternative]." |

---

## Anti-Patterns to Flag

**The Vanity Event:** High brand recognition but low ICP density. The team wants to go because "everyone goes." Score 1-2 on ICP density, 4-5 on strategic value. *Challenge:* "Would you still go if you couldn't post about it on LinkedIn?"

**The FOMO Event:** Competitors just announced they're sponsoring. Now leadership wants to match. No strategic rationale beyond "they're there so we should be." *Challenge:* "What's your differentiation story at this event? If you don't have one, you're paying to be a backdrop for their booth."

**The CEO Pet Event:** Founder loves this conference, attends every year, has friends there. Low ICP density, moderate budget. *Challenge:* "Is this an event or a networking trip? Both are fine — just don't pretend it's a pipeline play."

**The Default Event:** "We went last year so we're going again." No evaluation, no review of last year's results. *Challenge:* "What was your pipeline-to-cost ratio last year? If you can't answer that, you shouldn't re-commit without an evaluation."

**The Stretch Event:** Budget is clearly insufficient for the tier of presence needed. Team plans to "make it work" with a small booth in a bad location. *Challenge:* "If you can't afford to do this event well, the ROI will be worse than not going. A bad booth is worse than no booth."
