# Team Briefing Document Template

Use this template structure when building the event brief. Each section should be filled with event-specific data from the event-info.md, plan.md, and intake answers.

---

## Coverage Schedule Template

### Time Allocation Guide

| Activity | % of Event Hours | Who | Purpose |
|----------|-----------------|-----|---------|
| Booth staffing (rotating pairs) | 60% | Everyone, rotating | Conversations, demos, lead capture |
| Walking the floor | 15% | BDRs, floaters | Visit target accounts at THEIR booths, collect intel |
| Session attendance | 15% | Individual reps | Sit in talks where buyers are, network before/after |
| Networking / after-hours | 10% | AEs, leadership | Side events, dinners, hallway conversations |

### Rotation Schedule Grid

Build this based on team size and event hours. Standard format:

**Day 1 — [Date]**

| Time | Booth Anchor 1 | Booth Anchor 2 | Floater | Floor/Sessions |
|------|---------------|---------------|---------|----------------|
| 9:00-12:00 | [Name] | [Name] | [Name] | [Name] |
| 12:00-3:00 | [Name] | [Name] | [Name] | [Name] |
| 3:00-6:00 | [Name] | [Name] | [Name] | [Name] |

**Rules:**
- Two anchors at the booth at all times during expo hours. Non-negotiable.
- Rotate every 3 hours. Fresh energy = better conversations.
- Stagger lunch breaks — never leave the booth with fewer than 2 people.
- Floaters check in at the booth every 45-60 minutes to drop off leads and intel.
- If a session conflicts with booth coverage, booth wins. Send someone else to the session.

### For Small Teams (2-3 people)

- Designate one anchor, one floater
- Anchor stays at booth for 75% of time
- Floater alternates between booth and floor in 90-minute blocks
- Both attend evening events together

### For Large Teams (6+ people)

- Split into two crews: "morning crew" and "afternoon crew"
- Each crew has 2 anchors + 1 floater
- Off-duty crew handles meetings, sessions, and floor work
- Executive team has their own meeting schedule — don't count them for booth coverage

## Lead Scoring Rubric

### Standard 1-4 Scale

| Score | Profile | Conversation Quality | Example | Follow-up |
|-------|---------|---------------------|---------|-----------|
| **4 — Hot** | Decision-maker or strong influencer | Active pain, exploring solutions, budget allocated, timeline in next quarter | "We're evaluating vendors right now. Can you show me [specific thing]?" | AE personal email within 24 hours. Book demo immediately if possible. |
| **3 — Warm** | Right persona, right company size | Expressed interest, has the problem, but may not have budget or timeline | "This is interesting — we've been thinking about solving this but haven't started looking yet." | BDR personalized follow-up within 48 hours. Value-add content. |
| **2 — Lukewarm** | Relevant role but unclear fit | Had a conversation, asked questions, but no clear pain or urgency | "Cool product. I'll take a look at your website." | MAP nurture sequence. No personal outreach. |
| **1 — Badge scan** | Scanned badge or took swag | Minimal or no conversation | Grabbed a sticker, walked by | General nurture list only. |

### Scoring Rules

- **Score immediately** — within 60 seconds of the conversation ending. Memory fades fast.
- **Score UP when in doubt.** A false positive costs one extra email. A false negative costs a deal.
- **Add notes.** Score without notes is only half useful. Capture: what they care about, what you discussed, what you promised to send.
- **Flag existing customers.** Don't score a customer as a lead. Tag them as "customer" and route to CSM.
- **Don't let swag inflate scores.** Someone who grabbed a charger and answered one question is a 1, not a 2.

## Daily Huddle Agenda

**Duration:** 15 minutes max. Standing up. No chairs, no laptops open.

### Morning Huddle (30 min before expo opens)

1. **Today's pre-booked meetings** (2 min)
   - Who's meeting whom, when, where
   - Any prep needed (pull up their CRM record, review their site)

2. **Focus accounts** (2 min)
   - 3-5 target accounts to look for on the floor today
   - Who's assigned to each

3. **Adjustments from yesterday** (2 min)
   - What worked, what didn't
   - Coverage changes, rotation swaps

4. **Energy check** (1 min)
   - Anyone need a shorter shift? Different role today?
   - Remind: hydrate, eat, wear comfortable shoes

### Evening Huddle (after expo closes, at the booth or hotel lobby)

1. **Top 3 conversations** (5 min)
   - Who was it, what company, what do they care about
   - What's the next step
   - Should we change our approach based on what we're hearing?

2. **Hot leads needing same-day follow-up** (3 min)
   - Any score 4s from today?
   - Who's sending the follow-up email right now?

3. **Intel** (3 min)
   - What are competitors saying?
   - Any interesting trends from sessions or floor conversations?
   - Anything surprising?

4. **Tomorrow adjustments** (2 min)
   - Coverage changes
   - New focus accounts
   - "One thing I'll do differently tomorrow"

5. **Energy check** (2 min)
   - Team morale, logistics issues, travel problems
   - Dinner plans (keep the team together when possible)

## Same-Day Follow-Up Protocol

### Score 4 Leads — Within 4 Hours

**Who sends:** AE (the person who had the conversation, or the assigned AE if it was a BDR)

**Template framework:**
```
Subject: Great connecting at {Event} — {specific reference}

Hi {Name},

Really enjoyed our conversation at {Event} today — especially your point about {specific thing they said}.

As promised, {deliver on anything you committed to: case study link, intro to someone, pricing info}.

I'd love to continue the conversation and show you how we've helped companies like {proof point company} with {outcome}. Would {specific day/time next week} work for a 20-minute call?

{Sender}
```

**Rules:**
- Reference something SPECIFIC from the conversation. "Great meeting you" is lazy.
- Deliver on any promise made at the booth. If you said "I'll send you that case study" — do it now.
- Propose a specific time. Don't say "let me know when works."
- Send the LinkedIn connection request at the same time.

### Score 3 Leads — By End of Event Day

**Who sends:** BDR or AE who had the conversation

**Template framework:**
```
Subject: {Event} follow-up — {topic you discussed}

Hi {Name},

Glad we got to connect at {Event}. Your perspective on {topic} was really interesting.

Wanted to share {relevant resource: case study, blog post, or guide} that I think you'd find useful given what you mentioned about {their challenge}.

If you'd like to dig deeper into how we're helping {industry} teams with {outcome}, I'm happy to set up a quick call. Here's my calendar: {link}

{Sender}
```

### LinkedIn Protocol (Same Day for Score 3-4)

Connection request note:
```
Great meeting you at {Event} today, {Name}. Enjoyed our conversation about {topic}. Looking forward to staying connected.
```

## Demo at the Booth

### By Demo Length

**2-minute demo (recommended for most booth interactions):**
1. (0:00) "Let me show you the one thing that usually gets people excited."
2. (0:15) Show the single most impressive workflow — the "wow" moment
3. (1:15) "Companies like {X} saw {result} from this alone."
4. (1:45) "Want to see how this would work for your specific setup? Let's book a full demo."

**5-minute demo (for engaged prospects who want to see more):**
1. (0:00) "Based on what you told me, let me show you three things."
2. (0:30) Workflow 1 — their primary pain point
3. (1:30) Workflow 2 — secondary benefit
4. (2:30) Workflow 3 — differentiation moment
5. (4:00) "We helped {Customer} go from {before} to {after} in {timeframe}."
6. (4:30) "I'd love to do a full walkthrough tailored to your stack. Can we book 30 minutes next week?"

**10-minute demo (for serious prospects — pull them aside from main booth area):**
1. (0:00) 2 minutes of discovery: "Tell me about your current setup"
2. (2:00) 6 minutes of tailored walkthrough based on what they told you
3. (8:00) "What would this mean for your team if you could do this?"
4. (9:00) Next step: book a follow-up with their full evaluation team

### Demo Environment Rules

- Use a dedicated demo instance with clean, realistic data. Not your dev environment.
- Have an offline backup (video walkthrough, screenshots) in case WiFi fails.
- Demo on a large external monitor, not a laptop screen. People should be able to watch from a few feet away.
- Clear the demo between visitors. Don't let one prospect see another's data.
- Have one person demo, one person captures the lead. Don't try to do both.

## Competitor Intelligence

### How to Handle "How Are You Different From [Competitor]?"

**Framework:** Acknowledge → Differentiate → Redirect

1. **Acknowledge:** "They're a solid company — we respect what they do."
2. **Differentiate:** "Where we're different is [ONE crisp sentence about your differentiation]. For example, {Customer} switched from [Competitor] because [specific reason]."
3. **Redirect:** "But what matters more is whether we're the right fit for YOUR situation. Tell me more about [their challenge]."

**Rules:**
- Never trash talk. It makes you look insecure.
- Have ONE differentiator per competitor, memorized. Not five — one.
- If you don't know the competitor: "I'm not super familiar with them — what drew you to look at them?" Then listen. You'll learn more than they will.
- Don't get drawn into feature comparisons at the booth. "We'd need a real conversation to compare properly — let's book time for that."
