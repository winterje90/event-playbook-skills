# 60-Second Booth Talk Track Framework

## The Structure

You have 60 seconds to turn a passerby into a meeting. Here's how to use every second.

| Step | What to Do | Time | Key Principle |
|------|-----------|------|---------------|
| **Hook** | Ask a question about THEIR challenge | 5 sec | Pattern interrupt. Stop them in their tracks. |
| **Qualify** | 2-3 rapid questions to confirm ICP fit | 10 sec | Don't waste time on non-buyers. |
| **Bridge** | Connect their answer to a relevant outcome | 15 sec | Talk outcomes, not features. |
| **Proof** | One customer name, one metric | 15 sec | Specificity builds trust. |
| **Next Step** | Book a follow-up, right there | 5 sec | Don't let them "think about it." |

**Total: ~60 seconds.** If a conversation goes longer, great — you're having a real discussion. But the first 60 seconds determine whether that happens.

---

## Step-by-Step Detail

### Hook (5 seconds)

**Goal:** Stop foot traffic with a question that's impossible to ignore.

**Formula:** "Quick question — [problem they definitely have]?"

**Good hooks:**
- "Quick question — how are you handling [specific pain point] right now?"
- "Curious — are you seeing [industry trend] hit your team yet?"
- "We've been hearing a lot about [problem] from [their persona] — is that on your radar?"

**Bad hooks:**
- "Want to learn about [Company]?" — Nobody does. This is a pitch, not a hook.
- "What does your company do?" — Makes them do the work. And it's boring.
- "Can I scan your badge?" — Transactional and off-putting.
- "Want some swag?" — Attracts swag collectors, not buyers.

**The test:** Would YOU stop if someone asked you this? If not, try again.

### Qualify (10 seconds)

**Goal:** Confirm this person is worth your next 45 seconds.

**Three qualifying questions (ask 2-3):**
1. **Role:** "What's your role?" or "What team are you on?" — confirms they're the right persona
2. **Pain:** "How are you handling [X] today?" — confirms the problem exists for them
3. **Priority:** "Is that something your team is actively looking at?" — confirms timing

**If they don't qualify:**
- Be gracious: "Sounds like you've got it handled — enjoy the show!"
- Hand them a piece of swag or a one-pager
- Don't waste another minute. Move to the next person.

**If they DO qualify:** Transition to the bridge.

### Bridge (15 seconds)

**Goal:** Connect their specific pain to a relevant outcome — without pitching features.

**Formula:** "A lot of [their persona] we work with were dealing with exactly that. What we've found is [outcome they care about] by [how, at a high level]."

**Good bridges:**
- "A lot of VP Ops teams we work with were spending 20+ hours a week on that manually. We've helped them cut that to under 2 hours."
- "That's exactly what [Company X]'s team was struggling with. They ended up [achieving outcome] within their first quarter with us."
- "Interesting — we hear that a lot. The companies that have solved it are [doing X differently], and the results are pretty dramatic."

**Bad bridges:**
- Feature dumps: "So we have this dashboard that does X, and then there's the analytics module that does Y..." — Nobody cares about your features at this point.
- Company history: "So we were founded in 2019 and since then we've..." — Irrelevant.
- Jargon: "Our AI-powered platform leverages machine learning to..." — Stop.

### Proof (15 seconds)

**Goal:** Make the outcome believable with one specific example.

**Formula:** "[Customer name] was in a similar situation — [their problem]. Within [timeframe], they [specific result]."

**Good proof points:**
- "Acme Corp had the same problem — their team was losing 15 hours a week on manual reporting. Within 90 days, they had that down to 2 hours and redeployed three people to higher-value work."
- "[Customer] went from a 3-week onboarding process to 4 days. Their VP of Ops called it the biggest efficiency win they'd had in two years."

**Rules for proof points:**
- Name the customer (if allowed). Named proof is 3x more credible than "a customer."
- Use a specific metric. "Improved efficiency" means nothing. "Cut 15 hours to 2" means everything.
- Match the proof to their persona. If they're VP Sales, don't tell them about an Ops win.
- Have 3-4 proof points memorized for different personas.

### Next Step (5 seconds)

**Goal:** Get a commitment before they walk away. Don't let the conversation end open-ended.

**Good next steps:**
- "I'd love to show you how that would work for your team. Can I book you for a 20-minute demo next week?" (Pull out phone, open calendar)
- "Let me grab your info and send you that case study. Can I also set up a quick call to go deeper?"
- "We have a [speed consultation / live assessment] running at the booth — want to do a quick one right now?"

**Bad next steps:**
- "Here's my card, reach out if you're interested." — They won't.
- "I'll have someone follow up with you." — Too vague. When? Who?
- "Let me scan your badge." — Only if paired with a real next step.

**The rule:** Every conversation ends with either a booked meeting or a scored lead with notes. No exceptions.

## Talk Track Variants

### Standard (for Booth Anchors)
Full 60-second talk track as described above. This is the default for anyone staffing the booth.

### Abbreviated (for Floaters)
Floaters are working the floor — their job is to spark interest and funnel people to the booth.

**20-second version:**
1. Hook: Same opening question
2. Quick qualify: One question to confirm relevance
3. Redirect: "You should stop by our booth (#X) — we're doing [experience/demo]. I think you'd find it really relevant."

### Executive (for Leadership)
Executives don't pitch. They build relationships.

**Relationship version:**
1. "What's keeping your team busy this quarter?" (open-ended, peer-level)
2. Listen. Relate. Share a relevant observation from your own experience.
3. If there's a fit: "We should grab coffee — I think there's a lot we could compare notes on." (Book a side meeting, not a demo)

## Practice Protocol

**Before the event:**
- Each team member practices the talk track out loud 5 times. Not reads it — speaks it.
- Pair up and role-play: one person is the prospect, one delivers the talk track.
- Time it. If it's over 90 seconds, cut. Under 30 seconds, add more bridge.
- During the team briefing on-site, one volunteer delivers the talk track live. Team gives feedback.

**Common mistakes to correct:**
- Starting with the company name instead of a question
- Jumping to features before qualifying
- Forgetting the proof point
- Ending with "let me know if you're interested" instead of a firm next step
