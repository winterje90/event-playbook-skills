# Booth Strategy and Event Logistics

## Booth Design Principles for B2B SaaS

### Messaging Hierarchy

Your booth has about 3 seconds to communicate what you do and why someone should stop.

| Element | Purpose | Placement |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| **Hook** (largest text) | Stop foot traffic. Provocative question or bold claim. | Top of backdrop, visible from 20+ feet |
| **What you do** (secondary text) | One sentence. Category + outcome, not features. | Below the hook, visible from 10 feet |
| **Proof point** | Customer logo, metric, or quote. Builds instant credibility. | Mid-level, visible from 5-10 feet |
| **CTA** | "Book a demo" or "See how it works" | At eye level near the conversation area |

**Anti-patterns:**
- Walls of text that nobody reads
- Feature lists (save those for the conversation)
- Generic taglines like "Transform your business" or "The future of [category]"
- Your company history or founding story on the banner

**Tailor to the audience:**
- At industry-specific events: Skip the category explanation. Lead with your differentiator. Attendees already know the category.
- At broad conferences: Make what you do obvious from 10 feet away. Don't assume anyone knows your category.

### Before You Sign Off on Design

- [ ] Mock up your layout WITH furniture placement. Furniture blocks messaging — this catches teams every time.
- [ ] Check messaging hierarchy: Can someone understand your hook from 20 feet? What you do from 10 feet?
- [ ] Request printed proofs from the vendor. Colors look different on screen vs. on a 10-foot fabric banner.
- [ ] Verify height restrictions, fire codes, and booth regulations from the exhibitor manual.

## Booth Setup Checklist

### Equipment and Supplies

**Non-negotiable (bring or rent):**
- [ ] Monitor/screen for demos (32"+ recommended, bring your own HDMI cable)
- [ ] Laptop for demos (charged, with offline demo available)
- [ ] Portable WiFi hotspot (venue WiFi is unreliable — never depend on it for live demos)
- [ ] Power strip and extension cords (at least 2 — you'll always need more outlets than you think)
- [ ] Phone chargers (for the team, multiple cables)
- [ ] Business cards (yes, still — some people prefer them)
- [ ] Lead capture device (scanner, app, or shared Google Sheet)
- [ ] Pens and notepads (for jotting notes during conversations)
- [ ] Water bottles (for the team — staying hydrated keeps energy up)

**Booth supplies:**
- [ ] Printed collateral: one-pagers, case studies (keep it to 2-3 pieces max)
- [ ] Swag (see recommendations below)
- [ ] Tablecloth or branded table cover
- [ ] Bowl or container for business card collection (if running a drawing)
- [ ] Hand sanitizer (small, at the table edge)
- [ ] Tape, zip ties, scissors (you'll need at least one of these for setup)
- [ ] Cleaning wipes (screens get fingerprints during demos)

**Emergency kit:**
- [ ] Backup HDMI/USB-C adapters
- [ ] Extra phone charger
- [ ] Pain reliever / throat lozenges (talking for 8 hours is brutal)
- [ ] Breath mints
- [ ] Comfortable shoes (not joking — this matters)

## Swag Recommendations by Budget

### Under $500: Keep It Simple
- Branded stickers (high quality, die-cut) — $0.50-$1 each
- Pens (good ones, not the cheap kind that skip) — $1-$2 each
- Small notebooks — $2-$3 each

### $500-$2,000: Useful Items
- Portable phone chargers — $5-$10 each (most popular giveaway, gets kept and used)
- Reusable water bottles — $5-$8 each
- Quality t-shirts (soft, well-designed, people actually wear) — $8-$12 each
- Webcam covers — $1-$2 each (cheap, useful, tech-relevant)

### $2,000-$5,000: Premium Items
- Branded AirTag holders — $5-$8 each
- Quality hoodies — $20-$30 each (reserve for Tier 1 leads or side event guests)
- Premium notebooks (Moleskine-style) — $8-$15 each
- Custom socks (surprisingly popular) — $5-$10 each

### $5,000+: Experiences Over Objects
- Quiet VIP lounge with comfortable seating and good coffee
- Live demo bar with speed consultations
- Branded charging station (everyone needs one, you get foot traffic)
- Professional headshot booth (relevant for sales teams, always draws a crowd)

**The golden rule:** Useful > clever. Items that get used daily (chargers, water bottles, notebooks) give you ongoing brand impressions. Items that get thrown away before people leave the venue (foam fingers, branded sunglasses, stress balls) are a waste.

**Tie swag to conversation:** The best swag strategy is conditional — "fill out a quick assessment" or "book a demo" to get the premium item. This pre-qualifies the interaction. An unconditional giveaway attracts swag collectors, not buyers.

## Booth Engagement Tactics

Ways to pull people in that go beyond standing and smiling:

| Tactic | How It Works | Why It's Effective | Best For |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|----------|
| **Challenge wall** | Whiteboard asking "What's your biggest [problem]?" Attendees write answers. | Creates instant conversation starters and crowdsourced content | Brand awareness events, niche conferences |
| **Live assessment** | "Tell us your current setup, we'll score it in 2 minutes" | Delivers value AND qualifies the prospect simultaneously | Pipeline-focused events, $25K+ ACV |
| **Speed consultations** | 10-minute problem-solving sessions, bookable on the spot | High-value interaction that surfaces pipeline naturally | Mid-size events, consultative sales motion |
| **Interactive demo** | Self-guided demo on a tablet | Lets introverts engage without rep pressure | Product-led companies, tech-savvy audiences |
| **Results board** | Display live aggregate results from assessments or polls | Social proof + draws curiosity from passersby | Major conferences, high foot traffic |

**What doesn't work (usually):**
- Raffles/drawings with no qualifying step. You'll get a lot of entries and zero pipeline.
- iPad giveaways with no conversation required. You attract people who want iPads, not your product.
- Games that have nothing to do with your product. Fun but zero lead quality.

## Exhibitor Manual Review Checklist

The exhibitor manual is 20-40 pages and contains critical deadlines. Don't skim it.

**Extract and log these immediately:**

- [ ] **Electricity order deadline** — rarely included in booth package, almost always ordered separately
- [ ] **WiFi/internet order deadline** — dedicated line for demos is non-negotiable
- [ ] **Furniture rental deadline** — tables, chairs, counters, monitor stands
- [ ] **AV equipment deadline** — screens, monitors, microphones (if not bringing your own)
- [ ] **Lead scanner/badge reader rental deadline** — or check for app-based option
- [ ] **Shipping/drayage deadlines and instructions** — when to ship, where to ship, how to label
- [ ] **Setup and teardown schedule** — when you can access the venue, when everything must be cleared
- [ ] **Height restrictions and fire codes** — banner height limits, prohibited materials, required safety equipment
- [ ] **Booth graphic submission deadline** — early-bird vs. regular, file specs
- [ ] **Badge/pass count** — how many exhibitor badges are included, how to order more
- [ ] **Parking information** — for setup/teardown and daily access

**Drayage warning:** Drayage is the cost of moving your materials from the loading dock to your booth inside the venue. It is shockingly expensive ($50-$200+ per 100 pounds) and catches first-time exhibitors off guard every time. Budget 10-15% of your total booth build cost for drayage alone.

## Side Event Planning

### Customer Dinner (10-30 people)

**Timeline:**
- 8 weeks out: Identify venue, get quotes for 3 options
- 6 weeks out: Confirm venue, set menu, send invitations
- 4 weeks out: First RSVP deadline, send reminder to non-responders
- 2 weeks out: Final headcount to venue, collect dietary restrictions
- 1 week out: Final confirmation email with venue details, directions, start time
- Day of: Arrive 30 min early, confirm setup, brief your team on seating strategy

**Guest list mix (for a 25-person dinner):**
- 8-10 target prospects (Tier 1 TAL)
- 5-8 existing customers (mix of advocates and expansion targets)
- 3-5 partners or industry peers
- 4-5 of your team (CEO/founder, 2 AEs, 1 CSM, 1 marketing)

**Program flow:**
1. Cocktails/networking (30 min) — no assigned seating yet
2. Seated dinner — strategic seating (mix prospects with customers, not all your team at one end)
3. Brief welcome from CEO/founder (2-3 minutes max — not a pitch, just set context)
4. Conversation prompt or icebreaker (optional — only if the group needs it)
5. Dessert/coffee — open networking
6. Thank guests on the way out — mention any next steps casually

**Budget:** $150-$300 per head including venue, F&B, and gratuity. So a 25-person dinner runs $3,750-$7,500. Worth it if you get 3-5 qualified pipeline conversations — which you will.

### Happy Hour / Cocktails (30-60 people)

**Simpler logistics, wider reach:**
- Venue: bar or lounge near the conference venue with a private/semi-private area
- F&B: open bar + heavy appetizers for 2 hours ($75-$150/head)
- Invite broadly: TAL + customers + partners + interesting people you met at the booth
- Minimal programming: brief welcome, then let people network
- Have your team spread out — don't cluster together

### Workshop / Roundtable (15-25 people)

**Highest conversion rate, most effort:**
- Format: 60-90 minute facilitated discussion on a topic your ICP cares about
- Venue: conference meeting room or nearby hotel meeting space
- Content: real value, not a product pitch. Frame it as peer learning.
- Example: "How [Industry] Teams Are Rethinking [Topic]" — 20-min presentation, 40-min discussion
- Capture permission to follow up with the content/recording
- Budget: $500-$2,000 for venue + light refreshments

## Booth Location Strategy

**Prioritize:**
- Near high-traffic areas: entrances, food stations, main stage exits
- End-of-row positions (more visible than mid-row)
- Near complementary (not competitive) vendors
- On the main walkway between sessions and expo hall

**Avoid:**
- Corner booths in dead-end aisles
- Far from food/beverage areas
- Next to very loud or flashy booths that will drown you out
- Near restrooms (sounds odd but it's distracting)

**If you get a bad location:** Make up for it with aggressive floor work. Send floaters to high-traffic areas with swag and conversation starters. Don't let a bad booth location kill your whole event — adapt.
