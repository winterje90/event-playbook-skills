# Pre-Event Outreach Sequences

## Overview

The pre-event outreach sequence is where 40-50% of your qualified meetings come from. Teams that skip this and rely on "we'll just meet people at the booth" consistently underperform.

**Target:** 15-25 pre-booked meetings for major conferences, 5-10 for mid-size events.

**Who runs it:** BDRs for Tier 1 accounts, AEs for existing relationships and Tier 1 accounts where they have a connection.

**Account assignment:** Divide Tier 1 accounts (20-30) evenly across BDRs. Each BDR owns 8-15 accounts for the full sequence. AEs get their existing pipeline accounts plus 3-5 net-new Tier 1 accounts they have a LinkedIn connection with.

---

## The 6-Touch Sequence

### Touch 1 — Week 8: The Introduction (Email)

**Goal:** Introduce yourself, mention the event, plant the seed for a meeting.

**Subject line options:**
- `{Event Name} — worth connecting?`
- `Seeing you at {Event Name}?`
- `Quick question ahead of {Event Name}`

**Template:**

```
Hi {First Name},

I noticed {Company} will be at {Event Name} — we'll be there too, at Booth {#}.

We work with {similar companies / companies in their space} on {one-sentence value prop tied to their likely pain point}. Figured it might be worth a quick conversation while we're both there.

Would you be open to a 15-minute meeting during the event? Happy to work around your schedule.

{Sender Name}
{Title}, {Company}
```

**Personalization hooks:** Reference something specific — their company's recent news, a product launch, a mutual connection, or a trend in their industry.

---

### Touch 2 — Week 6: The LinkedIn Connection

**Goal:** Build the relationship on a second channel. Reference the email.

**Connection request note (300 char limit):**

```
Hi {First Name} — I reached out via email about connecting at {Event Name}. Would love to add you here and share some thoughts on {topic relevant to their role} ahead of the event.
```

**If already connected, send a LinkedIn message:**

```
Hi {First Name} — I dropped you an email last week about {Event Name}. We're helping companies like {reference company} with {outcome}. Would love to grab 15 minutes while we're both there. What does your schedule look like?
```

---

### Touch 3 — Week 4: The Value Add (Email)

**Goal:** Share something genuinely useful. Establish credibility. Soft meeting nudge.

**Subject line options:**
- `{Event Name} sessions worth your time`
- `What I'm watching at {Event Name}`
- `Quick {Event Name} prep`

**Template:**

```
Hi {First Name},

{Event Name} is a few weeks out — wanted to share a couple of things I think are worth your time:

- {Session/speaker recommendation relevant to their role}
- {Trend or insight relevant to their industry}

We've been working with {industry} teams on {specific outcome} and I'd love to hear how you're thinking about it. Any interest in a quick meetup at the event?

I'll have a few open slots — happy to make it easy: {Calendar booking link}

{Sender Name}
```

**What makes this work:** You're giving before asking. The session recommendation shows you understand their world. The insight shows you have relevant expertise.

---

### Touch 4 — Week 3: The LinkedIn Engagement

**Goal:** Stay visible. Mention something specific about their company.

**LinkedIn message:**

```
Hi {First Name} — saw {something specific: their recent post, company announcement, product update, funding round}. {One sentence connecting it to the event theme or your solution}.

Still have a couple of meeting slots at {Event Name} if you're interested. Either way, would be great to say hi at Booth {#}.
```

**Alternative (if they haven't responded to anything):**

```
Hi {First Name} — one more shot before {Event Name}. We're running {specific booth experience: live assessment, speed consultations, demo} at Booth {#}. {Companies like X} have found it valuable for {specific outcome}.

Stop by if you get a chance — even 5 minutes is worth it.
```

---

### Touch 5 — Week 2: The Calendar Push (Email)

**Goal:** Create urgency. Get the meeting booked.

**Subject line options:**
- `{Event Name} — last few meeting slots`
- `2 weeks out from {Event Name}`
- `Calendar filling up — {Event Name}`

**Template:**

```
Hi {First Name},

{Event Name} is in two weeks and my calendar is starting to fill up. Wanted to reach out one more time — I think a conversation about {specific topic relevant to them} could be really valuable.

I've got availability on:
- {Day 1 of event}: {Time slot}
- {Day 2 of event}: {Time slot}

If those don't work, just grab a time here: {Calendar link}

Looking forward to it.

{Sender Name}
```

**What makes this work:** Specific time slots reduce friction. "Calendar is filling up" creates urgency without being pushy.

---

### Touch 6 — Week 1: The Final Nudge (Email or LinkedIn)

**Goal:** Confirm existing meetings. Last-chance ask for non-responders.

**For confirmed meetings (Email):**

```
Hi {First Name},

Looking forward to our meeting at {Event Name} on {Day} at {Time}. Quick reminders:

- We'll be at Booth {#}
- I'll have my phone on me — text me at {number} if anything changes
- {One sentence about what you'll cover}

See you there!

{Sender Name}
```

**For non-responders (LinkedIn):**

```
Hi {First Name} — heading to {Event Name} this week. If you're there, stop by Booth {#}. We're doing {booth experience} and I think you'd find it relevant given {reference their role/industry}.

No pressure on a formal meeting — just wanted to make sure you know where to find us.
```

## Sequence Timing Summary

| Touch | Week | Channel | Purpose |
|-------|------|---------|---------|
| 1 | W8 | Email | Introduction + meeting ask |
| 2 | W6 | LinkedIn | Connection + email reference |
| 3 | W4 | Email | Value add + soft meeting nudge |
| 4 | W3 | LinkedIn | Personal touch + company-specific |
| 5 | W2 | Email | Calendar push + urgency |
| 6 | W1 | Email/LinkedIn | Confirm or last-chance |

## BDR vs. AE Assignment

| Account Tier | Who Sends | Why |
|-------------|-----------|-----|
| Tier 1 — no existing relationship | BDR | Cold outreach is BDR territory. AEs follow up on booked meetings. |
| Tier 1 — existing relationship | AE | AEs leverage existing trust. Don't let a BDR reach out to an AE's active contact. |
| Tier 1 — existing pipeline | AE | Event is an accelerator for deals already in motion. AE owns the conversation. |
| Tier 2 | BDR | Volume play. BDRs cover more ground. |
| Existing customers | CSM + AE | CSM identifies expansion opportunity, AE joins the conversation at the event. |

## Response Handling

| Response Type | Action |
|--------------|--------|
| "Yes, let's meet" | Book immediately. Send calendar invite within 2 hours. Add to shared team calendar. |
| "Maybe / not sure of schedule" | Reply: "Totally understand — I'll ping you the week of the event to see if a slot opens up." Add to Touch 6 for follow-up. |
| "Not attending" | Reply: "No worries — would you be open to a virtual conversation instead? We're seeing interesting things in {their industry}." |
| "Not interested" | Respect it. Remove from sequence. No follow-up at the event unless they come to the booth. |
| No response after 6 touches | Move to "booth drop-by" list. If you see their badge at the event, approach casually. No hard sell. |

## Measuring Outreach Success

Track these weekly during the sequence:

| Metric | Target |
|--------|--------|
| Emails sent | All Tier 1 + Tier 2 accounts reached |
| Open rate | 40%+ (if below, fix subject lines) |
| Reply rate | 15-25% (if below, fix personalization) |
| Meetings booked | 15-25 for major, 5-10 for mid-size |
| LinkedIn connection acceptance | 30-40% |
